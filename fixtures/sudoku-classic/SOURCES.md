# Fixture sources — sudoku-classic

Fixtures are research material. **No fixture ever enters `corpus/`**; every puzzle shipped in the
corpus is generated by this repository. Fixtures exist only to test the solver.

Every expected verdict in `fixtures/sudoku-classic/*.json` was established **here**, by the reference
brute force in `solver/reference/sudoku-classic.mjs`, or by a structural argument recorded in the
fixture's `note`. No verdict was copied from wherever a grid came from. Expected **bands** are a
different matter: a band is a property of this solver's own technique ladder, so each band was
recorded at calibration and frozen, so that a later change to the ladder fails the fixture test
instead of quietly reclassifying the corpus.

Rebuild with `node tools/make_fixtures.mjs sudoku-classic`. It is deterministic.

## constructed (18)

Written by argument, not taken from anywhere.

- `degenerate-empty-{4,6,9}` — an empty grid. Every valid completion solves it, so the verdict is
  `multiple` by definition.
- `degenerate-full-{4,6,9}` — a complete valid grid; the maximum clue count. Nothing is left to
  deduce, so it is `unique` trivially.
- `contradiction-row-{4,6,9}`, `contradiction-column-{4,6,9}`, `contradiction-box-{4,6,9}` — the
  same symbol twice in one unit. No completion can exist, so the verdict is `unsolvable`.
- `contradiction-altered-full-{4,6,9}` — a complete valid grid with one cell overwritten.

## generated (31)

Produced by this repository's own generator at a fixed seed, then classified by the reference.

- `unique-<size>-<boxH>x<boxW>-b<band>` — instances with a unique solution across every supported
  shape and every band the shape admits.
- `nearmiss-removed-…` — a unique instance with **one given removed**.
- `nearmiss-altered-…` — a unique instance with **one given changed** to a different symbol.
- `multiple-…` — a unique instance with a quarter of its givens removed, well past the point where
  the solution stops being forced.

## published (4)

Grids that circulate widely enough to be worth testing against. Each is named by where it is
commonly attributed, and each verdict was re-established here by the reference.

| Fixture | Where it comes from | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| `published-wikipedia-sample` | The sample grid in the Wikipedia article on Sudoku. | An ordinary 30-clue puzzle. |
| `published-inkala-escargot` | Widely circulated as Arto Inkala's "AI Escargot". | A hard grid; the solver puts it in band 5. |
| `published-seventeen-clue` | A seventeen-clue grid. Seventeen is the proven minimum clue count for a 9×9 with a unique solution (McGuire, Tugemann and Civario, 2012). | The minimum clue count. |
| `published-near-worst-case-backtracking` | A grid constructed to be slow for naive row-major backtracking, widely quoted as an anti-brute-force case. | The reference's worst case. It needs **69.2 million nodes and about 18 seconds**, while the solver finishes it with singles alone — it is band 1. This grid is why the reference's budget is sized at 120 million nodes, and it is the reason a budget overrun must be a rejection rather than a pass. |

Attribution above is *where a grid is commonly said to come from*, not a claim verified here. It has
no bearing on the fixture: the expected verdict comes from the reference either way.
