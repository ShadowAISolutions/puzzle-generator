# Fixture sources — binairo

Fixtures are research material. **No fixture ever enters `corpus/`**; every puzzle shipped in the
corpus is generated by this repository. Fixtures exist only to test the solver.

Every expected verdict in `fixtures/binairo/*.json` was established **here**, by the reference
brute force in `solver/reference/binairo.mjs`, or by a structural argument recorded in the
fixture's `note`. Expected **bands** are a different matter: a band is a property of this solver's
own technique ladder, so each band was recorded at calibration and frozen, so that a later change
to the ladder fails the fixture test instead of quietly reclassifying the corpus.

Rebuild with `node tools/make_fixtures.mjs binairo`. It is deterministic.

84 fixtures over grid sizes 6, 8, 10, 12 and 14. By verdict: 49 `unique`, 18 `unsolvable`,
15 `multiple`, and 2 where the reference could not finish inside its budget.

## constructed (21)

Written by argument, not taken from anywhere.

- `degenerate-empty-{6,8,10}` — an empty grid. Every valid board solves it, so the verdict is
  `multiple` by definition.
- `degenerate-full-{6,8,10}` — a complete valid board; the maximum given count. Nothing is left to
  deduce, so it is `unique` trivially.

Binairo has three rules, so it has three distinct ways to be impossible, and each is tested on its
own rather than bundled into one grid. This matters more here than the equivalent does for sudoku,
where every contradiction is ultimately the same rule about a repeated symbol:

- `contradiction-row-triple-{6,8,10}` and `contradiction-column-triple-{6,8,10}` — three of the
  same symbol side by side. Only the triple rule is broken. A solver that reasons purely from a
  three-cell window should refute these immediately, and the band-1 tier is exactly that window.
- `contradiction-row-unbalanced-{6,8,10}` — one more than half a line can hold, **spread out so no
  three ever touch**. The triple rule is satisfied everywhere; only the balance rule is broken.
  These are the fixtures that catch a solver which has quietly stopped checking a line once it is
  full. Three of them did exactly that on 2026-09-19, before the settled-line check went in.
- `contradiction-duplicate-rows-{6,8,10}` — two complete identical rows, taken from a valid board.
  Each row is legal by itself, no column holds three alike, and the counts are right everywhere.
  The only thing wrong is that the two rows are the same. This is the one rule no amount of
  single-line reasoning can see, which is why it earns its own fixture at every size.
- `contradiction-altered-full-{6,8,10}` — a complete valid board with one cell flipped, which
  unbalances both its row and its column at once.

## generated (63)

Produced by `generators/binairo/`, at sizes 6 to 14, with `none` and `rot180` given patterns,
targeting each band in turn. The generator's target is **not** the recorded band: every fixture
carries whatever the solver's trace actually said, which is why the set holds 13 band-1, 12
band-2, 13 band-3, 10 band-4 and 1 band-5 instance rather than an even 10 of each.

- `unique-<size>-<symmetry>-b<target>` (41) — instances with one solution, confirmed by the
  reference.
- `nearmiss-removed-*` and `nearmiss-flipped-*` (16) — a unique instance with one given taken
  away, and with one given flipped to the other symbol. Neither is assumed to be `multiple` or
  `unsolvable`: the reference decides, and the fixture records what it found.
- `multiple-*` (6) — a unique instance with a third of its givens removed, well past the point
  where the solution stops being forced.

## Two fixtures the reference could not finish

Two carry `"verdict": null` with `"reference": "budget-exceeded"`. They are kept deliberately. A
fixture the oracle cannot decide is still worth having, because what it tests is that the solver
**rejects** rather than waves through an instance nobody has confirmed. A timeout is a rejection,
never a pass, and these are where that is checked.

## Band 5 is rare here, and the set says so honestly

Only one band-5 fixture exists, at 12×12. Bands 1 to 4 were found readily at every size tried;
band 5 was not found at 6×6, 8×8 or 10×10 in any attempt. That is a fact about the family rather
than a gap in the search. Reaching band 5 means defeating cell-by-cell case analysis outright, and
on a small board almost anything with a single solution falls to it. `tools/make_plans.mjs` lists
band 5 only at the size where it was actually observed, and marks it scarce there rather than
promising it.

## No published fixtures

The sudoku set includes four widely circulated grids, used as research material. There is no
comparable body of canonical binairo instances to draw on, and inventing a provenance would be
worse than having none, so this family has none. Every fixture here is either constructed by
argument or generated by this repository, and each one says which in its `source` field.
