# killer-sudoku, built and waiting

This is a complete, tested killer sudoku family that is **not onboarded**. It generates nothing,
no puzzle of this family is in the corpus, and it is registered in no registry. It sits here rather
than under `solver/` because CI requires a passing `HARDENING/<family>.md` for every directory in
`solver/`, and `tools/harden.mjs` cannot produce one for this family. The argument for the change
that would unblock it is in `PROPOSALS/2026-09-19-family-agnostic-hardening.md`.

Nothing here is loaded by anything. The relative imports are written for the destination paths
below, not for this one, so the files do not resolve where they currently sit. That is deliberate:
it means no tool can pick them up by accident.

## What was measured while building it

All of these were run; none is an estimate.

- **Encoding.** `<size*size cage labels>|<fixed-width sums>`. A 9x9 with 22 to 38 cages encodes in
  126 to 158 characters, inside the schema's 256-character limit. The ceiling is 62 cages, which is
  what the label alphabet can name, and `encode()` refuses beyond it.
- **Bands.** All five are reachable on a 9x9, and the technique sets escalate as the ladder
  predicts: band 1 used only tier-1 rules, band 2 first reached `cage_innie_single` and the locked
  candidates, band 3 `cage_innie_set`, band 4 `cage_group_sum`, and `bounded_search` appeared in
  band 5 and nowhere else. Generation took 0.1 to 4.0 seconds per puzzle; a fresh classify took
  3ms at band 1 and 0.9s at band 5.
- **Duplicate detection.** The canonical form agrees **exactly** with a brute force over all
  3,359,232 orientations of a 9x9, on every instance tested, at 8.8ms against the brute force's
  13.8 seconds. It was wrong before that check: the search carried a comparison state down the
  recursion, and that state goes stale the moment a deeper leaf improves the running best. The
  brute force is what found it, which is the whole reason to write one.
- **The reference.** Confirms a 4x4 in 1ms, a 6x6 in 10ms, and a 9x9 in 14 to 250ms at band 1.
  At band 5 on a 9x9 it needs 2 to 5.5 seconds, and roughly one instance in four exceeds 40 million
  nodes and is therefore rejected rather than accepted unconfirmed. Filling cells cage by cage
  instead of in reading order is what made it finish at all: in reading order a sparsely caged 9x9
  did not complete inside 40 million nodes even at band 1.
