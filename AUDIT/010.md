## Drift

**None.** 200 records reclassified with the current solver: `band_drift: []`, zero problems, zero
duplicate hashes. `gate.mjs --all --render` passed **2,498/2,498** this batch, and the gate's
`banded` check re-derives band, score, `max_search_depth` and the full technique trace on every
record, so every Phase 0 record still classifies exactly as written.

CLAUDE.md step 4 says to compare the 200-record band distribution against the last audit and treat a
shift as evidence something frozen moved. The distributions differ:

| | b1 | b2 | b3 | b4 | b5 |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| audit 005 | 37 | 48 | 22 | 43 | 50 |
| audit 010 | 41 | 56 | 31 | 32 | 40 |

**That shift is composition, not drift, and the check is weaker than it looks.** The 200 are a
uniform shuffle of the whole corpus (`tools/audit.mjs:117`), so the expected counts follow the
corpus's own band shares, which changed deliberately between audits — band 3 went from 9.7% to
13.4% because batch 008 removed a plan-size cap that had been holding it down. Against the current
composition the sample gives **χ² = 7.74 on 4 degrees of freedom, below the 9.49 critical value at
5%**, so it is consistent with chance.

More to the point, a distribution comparison is only a proxy. The direct measurement is
`band_drift`, which is empty: every one of the 200 records reclassified to exactly the band and
score stored in it. If anything frozen had moved, records would reclassify differently, and that is
what this field reports. A future audit should read the distribution line as a weak signal and the
drift list as the real one.

## An independent solver, and what it establishes

Audit 005 hand-traced two puzzles. This audit does that too, but the stronger evidence came first: a
second solver written from scratch in the scratchpad, importing nothing from `solver/` or
`generators/`, implementing peer elimination, naked and hidden singles, locked candidates pointing
and claiming, naked and hidden pairs, triples and quads, and x-wing — and deliberately **no** tier-4
rule and **no** search.

That rule list was not guessed at. It is every technique name appearing in any band-1, band-2 or
band-3 record in the corpus, and nothing appearing only from band 4 up. Reading the vocabularies out
of the corpus gives the ladder directly:

| tier | techniques that first appear at this band |
|---|---|
| band 1 | `naked_single`, `hidden_single` |
| band 2 | `locked_candidates_pointing`, `locked_candidates_claiming`, `naked_pair`, `hidden_pair` |
| band 3 | `naked_triple`, `hidden_triple`, `naked_quad`, `hidden_quad`, `x_wing` |
| band 4 | `simple_coloring`, `swordfish`, `w_wing`, `xy_wing`, `xyz_wing` |
| band 5 | `bounded_search` |

**The vocabularies are strictly nested with no leakage across all 2,498 records.** No band-1 record
uses a tier-2 rule, no band-3 record uses a tier-4 rule, and `bounded_search` appears in band 5 and
nowhere else. `jellyfish` appears only at band 5, which is consistent — it is a tier-4 rule and the
records carrying it needed search as well.

Run over **60 records from each band**, spread deterministically across the whole corpus rather than
taken from the front:

```
band 1: 60 records -> solved by tiers 1-3: 60, stalled: 0, wrong/contradiction: 0
band 2: 60 records -> solved by tiers 1-3: 60, stalled: 0, wrong/contradiction: 0
band 3: 60 records -> solved by tiers 1-3: 60, stalled: 0, wrong/contradiction: 0
band 4: 60 records -> solved by tiers 1-3:  0, stalled: 60, wrong/contradiction: 0
band 5: 60 records -> solved by tiers 1-3:  0, stalled: 60, wrong/contradiction: 0
```

Every one of the 180 completions matched the record's stored `solution` exactly, and the propagation
never reached a contradiction or an unsound elimination on any of the 300. **The tier-3/tier-4
boundary is therefore confirmed by code that shares nothing with the solver**, which is a stronger
claim than two hand-traces, and it is cheap enough to repeat every audit.

The first run of this check stalled on 5 of 12 band-3 records, which looked like mis-banding. It was
not: those five all name `x_wing`, and the script did not yet implement it. That is worth recording
because the failure mode was mine, and the fix was to read the ladder out of the corpus instead of
assuming it from audit 005's examples.

## The two traces

### `2cf35b91ad095c3c` — band 4, 9×9, 30 clues, `max_search_depth: 0`

Recorded techniques: `naked_single`, `hidden_single`, `locked_candidates_claiming`, `hidden_pair`,
`xy_wing`. The claim to check is that tier 3 is not enough and that a tier-4 rule — the one named —
is what breaks it.

Tier 1–3 propagation stalls with **39 of 81 cells open**. At that stall:

- pivot `r7c8` holds `[27]`, pincer `r1c8` holds `[47]`, pincer `r8c7` holds `[24]`
- each pincer shares a unit with the pivot: `r1c8` down column 8, `r8c7` across row 8 and through
  the bottom-right box
- the pivot is 7 or 2. If 7, then `r1c8` must be 4. If 2, then `r8c7` must be 4. Either way a 4 is
  placed in one of the two pincers
- so 4 cannot sit in any cell seeing both, which is `r1c7` and `r6c8`

Deleting 4 from those two cells and nothing else, tier 1–3 propagation then completes the grid to
**0 cells open**, and the result matches the record's `solution` exactly. **Band 4 is honest**: one
tier-4 elimination is both necessary and sufficient here.

### `40bb5ed65de110c6` — band 5, 9×9, 26 clues, `max_search_depth: 3`

Recorded techniques: `naked_single`, `hidden_single`, `locked_candidates_pointing`,
`bounded_search`. Notably no tier-3 or tier-4 rule ever fires, so the record's own claim is that the
whole pattern ladder fails and search is what finishes it.

Tier 1–3 propagation stalls with **44 of 81 cells open**, and an exhaustive scan of that stall finds
**no xy-wing at all**, so the rule that rescued the band-4 puzzle is simply not available here.

Adding depth-limited bifurcation to the same independent propagation — guess the most constrained
cell, propagate, recurse — the puzzle needs **depth 4**. The record claims depth 3 with the full
tier-4 ladder in hand. That ordering is the right way round and is the reason the check is worth
running: a weaker deduction engine must need *at least* as much search as a stronger one. Had the
independent measurement come out below 3, the record would have been overstating its difficulty.
**Band 5 is honest** on the part that can be checked without reimplementing tier 4.

What this does not establish, stated plainly: the band-4/band-5 boundary itself rests on the
project's tier-4 implementation, because the independent solver does not implement those six rules.
The corpus-wide vocabulary nesting above is a consistency check on that boundary, not a proof of it.
A future audit that wants to close the gap should implement `swordfish`, `w_wing`, `xy_wing`,
`xyz_wing` and `simple_coloring` independently and expect every band-4 record to fall and every
band-5 record to stall.

## Uniqueness, checked a third way

The gate proves uniqueness with the project's solver and confirms it with the project's reference.
Both are the project's code. So the ten sampled records were also run through a deliberately dumb
exhaustive backtracker written for this audit — most-constrained-cell ordering, no technique ladder,
counting to a ceiling of two.

All ten returned **exactly one solution**, and in every case that solution equalled the record's
stored `solution` field. Corpus-wide, `duplicate_hashes` is empty across 2,498 records, and
`gate --all --render` passed 2,498/2,498 with `stored_where_it_belongs` true on every sampled
record, which also means no record's band lies: the band is part of the storage path, so a
mis-banded record would sit at the wrong path and fail `corpus-unique`.

Symmetry: mirror_h 26.8%, none 21.9%, mirror_v 18.8%, rot180 16.7%, diagonal 15.3%, rot90 0.4%.

The shape problem audit 005 raised is fixed, and the mechanism that fixed it — ranking plans by the
band/shape cell rather than the grid shape — is in `tools/make_plans.mjs` and needs no further
steering.

Two residual imbalances are **structural rather than neglect**, and a future audit should not file
repair tasks for them without reading this paragraph:

- **rot90 sits at 0.4% and cannot rise.** `make_plans.mjs` offers rot90 only where boxes are square
  and only at band 1, which is 4×4 and 9×9. The 4×4 cell is provably full at 5 records: rot90 splits
  16 cells into 4 orbits of 4, so only 15 clue patterns exist and only the 8-clue ones work. The 9×9
  band-1 rot90 cell holds 5 and is not capped, so that is the only place rot90 can grow.
- **band 3 is only reachable at 9×9.** `SUDOKU_SHAPES` lists band 3 for that shape alone, so band 3
  competes for a single shape's capacity while the other four bands draw on five or six. It has
  climbed from 9.7% to 13.4% and will keep climbing, but it cannot reach 20% without 9×9 dominating
  the corpus.

## Family balance, and why it scores 1 again

The corpus is still 100% `sudoku-classic`, and for the same reason as audit 005: the record schema
requires sudoku box dimensions on every puzzle, and the hardening suite can only build sudoku test
grids, so nineteen of the twenty rostered families cannot be onboarded. Both were measured in
batches 001 and 002 and argued in `PROPOSALS/2026-09-19-family-agnostic-params.md` and
`-family-agnostic-hardening.md`. The repair task filed by audit 005 is still open at
`queue/blocked/005-audit-repair-family-balance.md`, and three onboarding tasks sit in
`queue/blocked/` behind the same obstacle.

**The owner lifted this on 2026-09-19**, instructing that the corpus move to different families and
stop producing sudoku. That instruction, not this audit, is what unblocks the score. The next audit
should expect family balance to be the dimension that moves most, and should hold the new families
to the same standard this one applied to `sudoku-classic`: an independently written solver
reproducing the band boundary, an independent uniqueness count, and a rendering opened and read.

No dimension scored 2 or below other than family balance, which already has its repair task open, so
no new repair tasks are filed.
