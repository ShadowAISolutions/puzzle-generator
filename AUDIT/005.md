## The two hand-traces

The point of these is to confirm a band is honest rather than internally consistent, so both were
worked by hand and, for the second, against a candidate propagation written from scratch rather
than the solver's own.

### `a7b4cab0841dcedd` — band 1, 6×6, boxes 3 tall × 2 wide, 14 clues

Recorded techniques: `naked_single` only. Solved by hand start to finish, and **every single step
was a naked single** — a cell with exactly one candidate left given its row, column and box. In
order: r0c2=2 (row leaves {2,3}, column c2 already holds 3), r0c3=3, r1c5=3 (row leaves {1,3},
box holds 1), r1c0=1, r2c2=1, r2c3=6, r2c5=4, r2c4=2, r5c2=5, r5c3=2, r4c5=1, r5c5=6, r5c0=3,
r2c0=5, r4c0=2, r2c1=3, r4c1=5, r4c4=3, r3c1=4, r3c4=5, r5c1=1, r5c4=4.

No hidden single was ever needed, let alone a tier-2 rule. The completed grid matches the record's
`solution` field exactly. **Band 1 is honest.**

### `e0e52df8317eb423` — band 3, 9×9, 26 clues

Recorded techniques: `naked_single`, `hidden_single`, `locked_candidates_pointing`, `hidden_pair`,
`hidden_triple`. The tier-3 rule is `hidden_triple`, so the claim to check is that tier 2 is not
enough and a tier-3 rule is what breaks it.

A propagation implementing only tier-1 and tier-2 rules — peer elimination, hidden singles, locked
candidates pointing and claiming, naked pairs, hidden pairs — was written independently and run to
a fixpoint. It **stalls with 45 of 81 cells still open**, agreeing with
`deducibleWithin(puzzle, params, 2)` returning no band.

At that stall, column 7 reads:

```
r0c7 [1357]  r1c7 [57]  r2c7 [135]  r3c7 [36]  r4c7 [13]
r5c7 2       r6c7 [4689]  r7c7 [5689]  r8c7 [49]
```

Value 4 can only go in r6c7 or r8c7. Value 8 can only go in r6c7 or r7c7. Value 9 can only go in
r6c7, r7c7 or r8c7. Three values confined to the same three cells, so those cells hold exactly
4, 8 and 9 between them and nothing else: **r6c7 loses 6, r7c7 loses 5 and 6.** That is a hidden
triple, tier 3.

It is genuinely out of reach below tier 3. A hidden pair needs two values sharing an identical
two-cell set; here the three place-sets are {r6,r8}, {r6,r7} and {r6,r7,r8}, no two alike. A naked
pair needs a second cell whose candidates are exactly [49]; r6c7 and r7c7 each hold four. And the
tier-2 propagation had already run to a fixpoint without finding these eliminations.

Applying only that one triple and then resuming tier-1 and tier-2 rules takes the puzzle from
**45 open cells to 0**, completing to exactly the record's `solution`. **Band 3 is honest**: the
tier-3 step is both necessary and sufficient.

(The same triple also appears as a box-8 hidden triple, because those three cells share a box. It
is one deduction seen in two units, not two findings.)
